# Hardware

Every other document here configures software. This one configures **atoms** — the
watts, the bandwidth, the silicon and the cold water that a software house needs
before any of the engine runs.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Site** | Erkowit (أركويت), Red Sea Hills, eastern Sudan — ~4 h by road from Port Sudan |
| **Scale** | 7 seats, live-in hub |
| **Goal** | Own the stack — local model inference, Hermes, CRM, database, hosting, media generation |
| **Posture** | Off-grid by design, revenue-gated by tier, split-plane by blast radius |

> **Status: plan, not a purchase order.** Full build is ~$55K against a $5K runway.
> Only Tier 1 is affordable today. Everything else is costed so the trigger is a
> decision rather than a scramble — see [Tiers and triggers](#tiers-and-triggers).

## The site

| Fact | Value | Consequence |
| ---- | ----- | ----------- |
| Elevation | ~1,100–1,350 m (sources vary) | Ambient ~22 °C — **free-air cooling for the equipment room** |
| Distance to Red Sea | ~40–45 km | Salt-laden air → corrosion class C3/C4 on exposed metal |
| Distance to Port Sudan | ~90–95 km, ~4 h by road | Every spare part is a day trip; N+1 beats "buy the cheap one" |
| Nearest town | Sinkat, ~21 km of track | Fuel, labour, mobile signal reference |
| Fog | Heavy and frequent — a known **mist oasis** | PV derate + condensation risk + a free water source |
| Grid | Effectively absent; the national grid runs 12–18 h daily blackouts | Design **off-grid**; treat any grid as opportunistic top-up |

Erkowit is a British-era hill station, and the reason it *was* one is the reason it
suits us: it is the one place within reach of Port Sudan where you can think without
fighting the heat. That single fact is worth roughly $8–12K in avoided air
conditioning, PV and battery — and it is what makes hosting real compute here viable
at all.

**What the site charges in return:** logistics distance, no fibre, no grid, lightning
exposure on a hilltop, and fog that condenses inside anything left unsealed.

## What "run frontier models locally" can actually mean

This is the load-bearing constraint of the whole plan, so it goes first.

**Frontier models in the strict sense cannot be run locally by anyone.** Claude, GPT
and Gemini weights are closed — no amount of hardware buys them. What is genuinely
runnable is the **open-weight frontier-class tier**, and it is good, but it is not
level with what we use today:

> Self-hostable open-weight models top out around **71–72 % SWE-bench Verified**
> (Qwen3-Coder-Next, Devstral 2), while closed frontier coders sit at **80–95 %**.

So the honest architecture is a **ladder, not a replacement**:

| Lane | Runs on | Handles |
| ---- | ------- | ------- |
| **Hard lane** | Claude (Max plan, cloud) | Architecture, multi-step features, anything risky. Unchanged |
| **Volume lane** | **Local** open-weight model | Social drafting, CRM enrichment, summarisation, classification, translation, commit messages, doc passes, Hermes chat — the high-token/low-stakes work |
| **Offline lane** | **Local** | *Everything*, degraded, when the link is down — which in Sudan is a scheduled event, not a hypothetical |

That third row is the argument that money alone doesn't make. During Sudan's
nationwide blackout beginning February 2024, ~30 million people were offline for over
a month. A cloud-only team stops completely. A team with local weights, a local git
mirror and local CI keeps shipping at reduced quality. **That is the real reason to
own inference here** — the token savings are a bonus.

### The trap in the "671B on a Mac" headline

A Mac Studio M3 Ultra with 512 GB unified memory can hold DeepSeek-R1 671B at 4-bit
(~404 GB) and generate at ~17–18 tok/s under 200 W. It is a genuinely astonishing
number and **it is the wrong model for our workload**: reported prompt processing is
on the order of **14 minutes to ingest an ~8k-token prompt**. Agentic coding sends
20–100k tokens of context on every turn. Generation speed is not the bottleneck —
prefill is.

**Therefore: size for a 80B-class coder with fast prefill, not a 671B trophy.**
Qwen3-Coder-Next 80B runs in ~45 GB with a 256K context — that fits comfortably and
responds at conversational speed.

| Model class | Weights (4-bit) | Fits on | Verdict |
| ----------- | --------------: | ------- | ------- |
| 20–30B coder | 12–18 GB | Any 24 GB GPU, 64 GB Mac | Fast, fine for the volume lane |
| **80B-class coder** | **~45 GB** | **256 GB Mac / 96 GB GPU** | **The target.** Best quality that still prefills fast |
| 235B MoE | ~120–140 GB | 256 GB Mac / 2× 96 GB GPU | Possible; prefill starts to hurt |
| 671B MoE | ~404 GB | 512 GB Mac only | Trophy. Unusable prefill for agentic work |

## Three planes, split by blast radius

"All owned local" is right for most of the stack and wrong for one part of it. The
dividing line is not ideology — it is **who is harmed when the hilltop link drops**.

| Plane | Where it runs | Why |
| ----- | ------------- | --- |
| **Internal plane — move it all local** | Erkowit | Inference, Hermes gateway, CRM, internal Postgres, git mirror, CI runners, dev + staging environments, package cache, media generation, monitoring, backups. If the link drops, *we* are inconvenienced. Nobody else notices |
| **Customer plane — stays cloud** | Vercel + Neon + CDN | Live school tenants (Albayan, King Fahad, Aldar). A school's SLA cannot be backed by a hilltop on an LTE link 4 h from a technician. Owning this would be negligence, not sovereignty |
| **Never local** | Anthropic | The hard lane. See above |

**The customer plane migrates only on evidence** — 12 months of measured site uptime,
and then only for customers who *want* on-prem. Which some do: the
[delivery](/docs/delivery) doc already sells a self-hosted and an on-prem tier to
Tier-1 procurement. That reframes this entire capex: **the Erkowit stack is the
reference implementation of the on-prem enterprise product**, not just an internal
cost saving. It is the only argument here with a direct line to revenue.

## The compute nodes

Four nodes, separated by **duty cycle** rather than by task — because on solar,
*when* a watt is consumed matters as much as how many.

### Node A — always-on inference

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Hardware** | Mac Studio M3 Ultra, 256 GB unified memory |
| **Power** | ~15 W idle, ~200 W under load → **~1.5 kWh/day** |
| **Serves** | 80B-class coder + a small fast model, behind one OpenAI-compatible endpoint |
| **Why Apple** | Watts per token. An 8× H100 box doing the same job is ~7 kW continuous — **168 kWh/day**, six times this entire compound's design load, before its cooling. Apple silicon is the only tier that fits an off-grid energy budget at all |

Routed through LiteLLM so every kun agent can address it as one more model lane
alongside the existing `engine.json` ladder.

### Node B — solar-window burst (media + heavy contexts)

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Hardware** | NVIDIA workstation — RTX 5090 32 GB (budget) or RTX Pro 6000 Blackwell 96 GB (capable) |
| **Power** | ~850 W under load, off otherwise → **~4.2 kWh/day at 5 h** |
| **Serves** | Image generation, video generation, large-context code jobs, batch reprocessing |
| **Scheduling** | **Runs in the solar window only.** Jobs queue outside it |

Image generation is comfortable: FLUX-class models run well inside 24–32 GB. Video is
where ambition meets physics:

| Local video model | Quantised VRAM | Time per clip on RTX 5090 |
| ----------------- | -------------: | ------------------------- |
| LTX-Video 2.3 | modest | **~4 s** — near real-time |
| HunyuanVideo 1.5 FP8 | 8–16 GB | 10–15 min |
| Wan 2.2 14B FP8 | ~24–32 GB (54–65 GB at full precision) | 8–12 min |

So local video is **feasible for volume, not for hero assets**. Quality still sits
below Veo-class output. The rule: **local for drafts, iterations and the long tail;
cloud (Higgsfield / Google) for the one asset a client actually sees.** The
[higgs](/docs/media) lane keeps both renderers and picks per job — exactly the
dual-renderer pattern it already implements.

### Node C — the server plane

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Hardware** | Compact Ryzen/EPYC server, 128 GB ECC, 2× NVMe mirrored + 4× HDD |
| **Power** | ~85 W average → **~2.0 kWh/day** |
| **Runs** (Proxmox / Docker) | CRM · Hermes gateway · PostgreSQL · Redis/Valkey · git mirror · CI runners · staging environments · package cache · monitoring · backup target |

Notes that make this easy rather than heroic:

- **The CRM is small.** Twenty needs ~750 MB RAM idle across its four services
  (server, worker, Postgres, Redis); 2 cores / 2 GB handles a small team, 4 GB is
  comfortable. Version 2.1.0 (April 2026) is the first release marked production-ready
  for single-tenant self-hosting. Our four workspaces are a rounding error on this box.
- **Hermes needs no inbound port.** Slack Socket Mode (`xapp` token) is outbound-only
  — which is exactly right behind CGNAT on an LTE link. This is the single
  best-behaved service in the plan.
- **Not a used enterprise rack.** A second-hand 2U idles at 200–400 W and is loud. On
  solar, idle power is the whole game — buy efficient, not cheap.

### Node D — the seats

7 laptops the team already owns, 5 external monitors, wired ethernet to every desk.
**Wi-Fi is not a link you want to debug at 1 a.m.**

## Solar-scheduled compute

The design principle that makes owning this affordable.

On an off-grid site, **a daytime kWh costs roughly a third of a night-time kWh**:

```
Daytime load   → needs PV only
                 1 kWp ≈ 3.4 kWh/day at 4.5 PSH × 0.75 derate, ≈ $350 landed
                 ≈ $103 per kWh/day of capacity

Night-time load → needs PV *and* battery to carry it
                 + ~1.2 kWh of LiFePO4 at ~$220/kWh
                 ≈ $370 per kWh/day of capacity
```

Therefore: **oversize the array, right-size the battery, and move every flexible load
into the sun.** Flexible here means media renders, batch inference, CI, backups, the
water pump, and laundry.

kun already has the machinery for this. The social pipeline is a **queue with a cron
drain** — the draft-drain job is live today. Making that drain solar-aware is a
scheduling change, not an architecture change:

> Render the queue when the battery is above 70 % and the array is above 60 % of
> nameplate. Otherwise let it wait.

That one rule is worth more than several kWh of battery.

## Electrical sizing

### Battery

```
E_batt = (E_daily × A) / (DoD × η_inv)

  E_daily = 28 kWh/day    A (autonomy) = 1.2 days
  DoD     = 0.90 (LiFePO4) η_inv       = 0.94

E_batt = (28 × 1.2) / (0.90 × 0.94) = 39.7 kWh  →  40 kWh nameplate
```

**Specify: 48 V LiFePO4, 40 kWh, as 8 × 5 kWh rack modules on a common BMS bus.**

Modules, not a monolith — a dead module costs 5 kWh, not the bank, and one module
fits in a car for the trip to a technician. At full load this is 1.2 days; on the
firm bus alone with everything else shed, it is **closer to five days**, which is the
autonomy figure that actually matters.

At 48 V the DC side is heavy: 15 kW of inverter is **>300 A**. Use a proper busbar,
keep battery-to-inverter runs under 2 m, and fuse every string with a Class T. This
is where cheap installs burn down.

### Solar array

```
P_pv = E_daily / (PSH_worst-month × derate)

  PSH_worst-month = 4.5 h   (Sudan GHI ≈ 6.0–6.5 kWh/m²/day; Erkowit fog
                             and mist justify a conservative worst month)
  derate          = 0.75    (soiling, temperature, wiring, MPPT, mismatch)

P_pv = 28 / (4.5 × 0.75) = 8.3 kW minimum
```

**Specify: 16 kWp** — roughly 2× the minimum, about 29 × 550 W modules, ~80 m².

The oversizing is deliberate and it is the cheapest decision in the document. At
$0.25–0.45/W the marginal panel is the cheapest energy in the system, and it buys:
fog-day resilience, headroom to run Node B without touching the battery, ~0.5 %/yr
degradation cover, and growth to a 9th and 10th seat without a second procurement
trip. On a clear day a 16 kWp array makes ~54 kWh — enough to run the media queue
*and* fill the bank.

Altitude helps: cell temperature is the dominant PV loss in Sudan, and 1,350 m runs
8–12 °C cooler than Khartoum, so real yield should beat the 0.75 derate. Fog works
the other way. Pull the exact pin from
[Global Solar Atlas](https://globalsolaratlas.info) before ordering.

Tilt ~15–18° south is optimal, but **use ≥20° regardless** — fog and dust need a
self-shedding angle. Mounting must be wind-rated for an exposed hilltop.

### Inverters

Peak concurrency: Node B 0.9 + Node A 0.25 + server 0.15 + seats 0.5 + pump start 2.2
+ kitchen 2.0 + fans/lights 0.6 ≈ **6.6 kW, surging to ~8.5 kW**.

**Specify: 3 × 5 kW 48 V hybrid inverters in parallel (15 kW).**

Not one big unit. At 4 hours from a replacement, N+1 is the whole argument: one dead
inverter in a parallel trio is a degraded week; one dead inverter in a single-unit
design stops the company. The trio also gives the MPPT capacity 16 kWp needs and lets
Tier 2 start with one and grow.

### Priority bus

Split the distribution board into two circuits from day one:

| Circuit | Loads | Behaviour |
| ------- | ----- | --------- |
| **Critical** | Node C, network, storage, Node A, one light circuit, fridges | Never shed. Protected autonomy ~5 days |
| **Flexible** | Node B, pump, laundry, fans, kitchen, monitors | Shed first; scheduled into the solar window |

This is what makes a 40 kWh bank behave like a much larger one.

## The UPS mostly disappears

A conventional office buys a UPS because the grid drops. Here there is no grid to drop
from: the hybrid inverters plus 40 kWh of battery **are** the UPS, and every laptop
carries its own second layer.

What is still worth buying: **one 1 kVA online UPS on the critical rack** — Node C,
network, Node A — so inverter faults, transfer events and maintenance never hard-cut
a database mid-write. Anything beyond that duplicates a bank you already paid for.
Put the savings into protection, which is what actually determines whether this
equipment sees its second year.

## Protection and environment

A hilltop at 1,350 m, 40 km from salt water, inside a mist oasis. Four hostile
conditions, four cheap countermeasures.

| Threat | Countermeasure |
| ------ | -------------- |
| **Lightning** — exposed high ground | Earth rod array to <10 Ω, bonded to array frames, mast and enclosures; Type 1+2 SPD on AC, Type 2 on DC strings. Non-negotiable, and the cheapest insurance in the BOM. Measure soil resistivity on the survey — rocky hills often need multiple rods or a bentonite trench |
| **Condensation / fog** | IP54+ enclosures with breathers; dehumidifier in the equipment room; **no outdoor-exposed electronics**. Fog here is daily weather, not an exception |
| **Salt + corrosion** | Aluminium or hot-dip galvanised mounting, stainless fixings, annual torque inspection. Painted steel fails within a few seasons |
| **Dust / haboob** | Panel cleaning monthly, weekly after dust events; sealed cable glands; filtered intake on the equipment room. Soiling losses hit 10–20 % when this slips |

## Cooling

The site's climate is why this section is short — and why hosting compute here works
at all.

**No general air conditioning.** At ~22 °C ambient the correct design is envelope and
airflow: thermal mass, shaded openings, cross-ventilation, ceiling fans, insulated
roof. Eight fans at 3.2 kWh/day replace a split-AC load of 8–14 kWh/day — about 4 kWp
of PV and 10 kWh of battery not purchased, on the order of $5–8K.

**The equipment room is the exception, and free-air cooling handles it.** Nodes A, B
and C plus inverters put roughly 0.5–1 kW of heat into that room while working. In
Khartoum that means a compressor. At 22 °C ambient it means **filtered forced
ventilation** — two EC fans, ~200 W, ~1.6 kWh/day, versus ~6 kWh/day for a mini-split
doing the same job.

Two caveats: the intake must be **filtered** (dust) and **dehumidified or warmed**
(fog), and a small mini-split should still be installed as backup for the hottest
weeks and for the day Node B runs flat out. Sized for backup duty, not baseline.

## Connectivity

Power is solvable with arithmetic. The link is genuinely hard, and it decides whether
the site is viable.

| Option | Legal status | Cost | Latency | Verdict |
| ------ | ------------ | ---: | ------- | ------- |
| **LTE via mast + directional antenna** | Licensed retail service | $600–1,200 capex + plan | 40–80 ms | **Primary.** Altitude is the asset — a 12 m mast on a 1,350 m hilltop reaches corridor cell sites well past normal CPE range |
| **Licensed VSAT** (TPRA-licensed — NTvsat, Hotnet, GlobalTT, Vizocom) | Licensed | $1,500–3,500 + $200–800/mo | ~600 ms GEO | **Backup.** Legal and works anywhere, but costs ~4.3 kWh/day and makes interactive sessions sluggish |
| **Point-to-point microwave to Sinkat / Port Sudan** | Needs a licensed partner | Site-dependent | 5–20 ms | Best case if a partner with licensed backhaul exists. Investigate on the survey |
| **Starlink** | **Not licensed in Sudan.** Widely used informally since the 2024 network collapse | — | ~40 ms | Do not build the plan on it. Real legal and continuity exposure for a company signing school contracts. Revisit if regulatory status changes |

**Design: dual-WAN failover router, LTE primary, licensed VSAT secondary**, VSAT
powered down (not standby) unless primary is out — that is what keeps 4.3 kWh/day off
the load table. Two SIMs on different carriers (Zain, MTN, Sudani) is the cheapest
redundancy available and should be done first.

### Ingress — serving anything from behind CGNAT

Rural LTE gives no static IP, no inbound ports, and slow upload. Every locally-hosted
service must therefore reach the world **outbound-only**:

| Service | Ingress method |
| ------- | -------------- |
| Hermes gateway | **Slack Socket Mode** — outbound WebSocket, no public endpoint. Already correct |
| CRM, internal dashboards, staging | Cloudflare Tunnel or Tailscale — outbound-only, no port forwarding |
| Customer production | **Not served from here.** See [three planes](#three-planes-split-by-blast-radius) |

Upload bandwidth, not download, is the ceiling on anything self-served. Measure it on
the survey.

### Bandwidth is the real scarcity

The least obvious and highest-leverage item in the plan.

**Agent traffic is cheap.** A Claude session is text — order of a few MB per hour.
Local inference makes it cheaper still. What actually eats the link is developer
housekeeping:

| Traffic | Typical size | × 7 devs |
| ------- | -----------: | -------- |
| `pnpm install` on a large repo | 0.8–1.5 GB | tens of GB/month |
| Docker image pulls | 0.5–2 GB each | bursty, large |
| macOS / Xcode updates | 6–12 GB each | catastrophic on a metered link |
| **Model weights** | **40–400 GB each** | **Plan downloads as events, not commands** |

That last row is new with local inference and it is not small. Pulling an 80B model
over a metered link is a multi-day, multi-hundred-dollar operation. **Model weights
should arrive on physical media from Port Sudan**, not over the air — and Node C
should keep every downloaded weight file on the NAS permanently.

Node C therefore earns its watts by running a shared cache: pnpm/npm mirror, git
mirror of the databayt repos, an OS-update staging area where one machine pulls and
six take it off the LAN, a model-weight archive, and nightly encrypted offsite backup
scheduled into the idle hours. On a metered link this box is the difference between a
workable opex and an unworkable one.

## Tiers and triggers

Nothing is bought before its trigger fires. The Drive settles it: cash flow first, and
a compound that eats the runway before there is revenue is the failure mode, not the
plan.

| Tier | What | Capex | Trigger |
| ---- | ---- | ----: | ------- |
| **0 — Today** | Laptops + cloud. No site | $0 | Current state |
| **1 — Blackout resilience** | Per person in Sudan: 1 kWh portable power station + 200–400 W folding panel + LTE router + surge strip. Buys 8–10 working hours through an outage | **$400–900/person** | **None — do this now.** Directly protects delivery against 12–18 h daily outages, affordable inside current burn |
| **2 — Anchor node + server plane** | 5 kWp PV, 15 kWh battery, 1 × 5 kW hybrid, mast + LTE, **Node C**. CRM, Hermes, git mirror, cache, staging all move local. 2 seats | **$10,000–15,000** | **First paying school**, or non-dilutive funding |
| **3 — Local inference** | **Node A** + PV to 12 kWp + battery to 30 kWh + 2nd inverter | **$10,000–15,000** | **3 paying schools / ~$3K MRR sustained 3 months** — burn covered by revenue *before* capex |
| **4 — Full hub + media lane** | **Node B**, 7 seats, housing, PV to 16 kWp, battery to 40 kWh, 3rd inverter | **$15,000–30,000** | Media volume measurably justifies it — see below |
| **5 — Customer plane migration** | Selected tenants on owned iron | Incremental | **12 months of measured site uptime** *and* a customer who wants on-prem and pays for it |

### Payback, honestly

| Tier | Replaces | Real payback |
| ---- | -------- | ------------ |
| **2 — server plane** | CRM/staging/dev hosting, ~$50–150/mo | 20–60 months on cost alone. **The actual return is offline continuity + the on-prem product reference** |
| **3 — inference** | Volume-lane token spend; offsets ~3 Max seats (~$300/mo) if it absorbs the cheap lane | ~22–30 months including power. Reasonable |
| **4 — media node** | Higgsfield credits + Google per-image spend | **Measure first.** If current media spend is ~$50–100/mo, a $5K GPU is a 4–8 year payback and **does not justify itself on cost**. It justifies itself only if media volume becomes a growth lever, or if offline capability is load-bearing. Do not buy this on enthusiasm |

**Fund Tiers 2–4 non-dilutively first.** A rural, off-grid, renewable-powered
engineering hub in eastern Sudan running open-weight AI is a strong story for exactly
the grants, vendor-credit programmes and accelerator tracks already on the fundraising
ladder. Spending seed runway on copper, lithium and silicon should be the last option.

**A generator is deliberately absent.** Fuel scarcity and price are precisely why
Sudanese households are moving to solar; a diesel genset imports the problem the site
was chosen to escape. If one is ever bought it is a manual-start emergency unit, never
part of the daily energy balance.

## Risks

| Risk | Severity | Mitigation |
| ---- | -------- | ---------- |
| **Security / theft** | High | A remote compound holding ~$25K of copper, lithium, panels and GPUs is a target. Permanent caretaker presence, physical enclosure, community relationship — budget it, don't improvise it |
| **Conflict exposure** | High | Red Sea State is comparatively stable and Port Sudan is the de facto seat of government, but this is a live-conflict country. The plan must survive evacuation: keeping the customer plane in the cloud means the *business* survives even if the *site* does not. This is the strongest argument for the split |
| **Team concentration** | Medium | 7 people in one remote location is a continuity question, not a hardware one. Keep at least one person outside the site |
| **Link failure** | High | Dual carrier + licensed VSAT. Local inference reduces but does not remove the dependency |
| **Buying Node B on enthusiasm** | Medium | Measure media spend for 3 months first. See payback table |
| **Lightning** | Medium | Earthing and SPDs as specified |
| **4 h from a hospital** | High | First-aid capability and a reliable vehicle are prerequisites, not accessories |
| **Import / customs overrun** | Medium | Assume the upper end of every range; verify landed cost with a Port Sudan broker before committing. GPUs and lithium both attract scrutiny |

## Site survey checklist

Nothing above is buyable until someone stands on the hill. The first visit returns
with:

- [ ] **LTE readings** — RSRP/RSRQ/SINR for Zain, MTN and Sudani at roof height on each bearing, **plus upload speed**. This single measurement decides viability
- [ ] **Grid** — any village supply? Voltage, hours per day
- [ ] **Building** — condition, roof load capacity, room count, lockable equipment room, photographs
- [ ] **Ground** — soil resistivity for earthing; rock depth
- [ ] **Water** — nearest trucked supply, cost per m³, delivery frequency, road access in rain
- [ ] **Wind** — prevailing direction and observed extremes, for array and mast rating
- [ ] **Fog** — hours per day, season, how late into the morning it persists
- [ ] **Road** — real travel time and vehicle requirement to Sinkat and Port Sudan
- [ ] **Local labour** — is there an electrician who can be trained as caretaker?
- [ ] **Ownership** — who controls the building, on what terms

## Open decisions

1. **Rent, renovate, or build?** Costed as renovation. Needs the survey.
2. **Node B: 32 GB or 96 GB?** 32 GB runs quantised video at 480p; 96 GB runs it
   properly and doubles as a fast-prefill code node. A ~$7K difference — decide after
   measuring three months of media spend.
3. **Is microwave backhaul real?** Depends on a licensed partner in Sinkat or Port
   Sudan. Would beat both LTE and VSAT.
4. **Does the hub replace remote work or supplement it?** The hardware answer is the
   same; the continuity answer is not.
5. **Who owns the site?** DevOps is Sedon's lane, but so is Saudi operations. Needs a
   named owner before Tier 2 fires.
6. **Equipment insurance** — obtainable in Sudan at a sane price? If not, the theft
   risk is uninsured and the caretaker line grows.
