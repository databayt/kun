# Email

Databayt's client mail still leaves from personal inboxes. This page is the architecture for
replacing that with company-owned `hi@` and `sales@` on every product domain, plus the tracer
that keeps the domains and mail records alive. **It is a research document first: nothing in
production DNS or any mailbox changes until the one-domain test in [§19](#19-migration-plan)
passes and Abdout approves.**

Labels used throughout:

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Verified** | Read on the vendor's official page (link given), checked 2026-10-03 |
| **Recommendation** | Our architectural choice |
| **Assumption** | Believed true, not confirmed by an official source |
| **Must-test** | Only the lab test in §19 can settle it |

## 1. Executive summary

- **Domains:** `databayt.org` (company), `balqalam.com` (Hogwarts), `mkan.sd` (Mkan), and one
  upcoming product. All four sit on Cloudflare DNS. The registrars are Namecheap, Cloudflare and sdnic.
- **Apple iCloud+ has the capacity but fails the ownership requirement.** One iCloud+ plan
  allows 5 domains and 3 addresses per person per domain, so all 8 addresses fit. But a company
  Apple Account shared by four salespeople breaks the iCloud Terms: *"You should not share your
  Account and/or password details with another individual"*. Giving addresses to employees'
  own Apple Accounts instead means that, after they are removed, *"any emails they sent and
  received… remain in their iCloud Mail account"*. Apple's 2026 **Apple Business** email is
  US-only and allows one domain per organization.
- **Microsoft Exchange Online Plan 1 is the recommended draft** (Recommendation). It costs $4 per
  user per month. One tenant holds every domain. `hi@` and `sales@` become **company-owned
  shared mailboxes**, which are free up to 50 GB. People get, and lose, *permission* to them,
  and the mail never leaves with a person. Apple Mail on Mac and iPhone connects natively.
  The core setup costs **about $240 a year**.
- **Google Workspace Business Starter is the close alternative.** It costs $7 per user per
  month, and Google Groups serve as free collaborative inboxes. Choose it if the team lives in
  Gmail, Docs and Meet.
- **Outreach never goes out from `hi@` or `sales@`.** Cold sequences run on a separate domain
  through a dedicated tool. App mail stays on Resend on the `send.` subdomain, which is already
  the case for databayt.org.
- **Twenty stays the system of record.** The mailbox is only a channel. Twenty v1.3+ syncs
  Google, Microsoft and IMAP mailboxes, but only *true mailboxes* (see §10).
- **Health tracing is live** (see [Health tracing](#health-tracing)). Every domain's expiry,
  auto-renew, nameservers, MX, SPF, DKIM, DMARC and TLS are checked daily, and the inboxes
  weekly. Problems open an assigned GitHub issue that names the fix.

## 2. Databayt requirements

1. A professional identity: `hi@` and `sales@` on each of four domains.
2. Reliable delivery in both directions, with mail never lost.
3. Mail and identity owned by the **company**. A departing employee loses access the same day
   and takes nothing with them.
4. Apple Mail on macOS, iPhone and iPad as the daily client.
5. A small sales team (about 4) working human-in-the-loop, out of about 20 staff in total.
6. **Twenty CRM** as the authoritative customer record.
7. Low cost: cash flow comes first ([The Drive](/docs/captain)).
8. Room for the existing automation: Mail.app scripting, plus read/send loops like the jobs lane.

**Not** a requirement: an enterprise collaboration suite. Twenty, Slack and the kun engine
already cover collaboration.

## 5. Company ownership model

Four separate layers. Most email incidents come from blurring them.

| Layer | What it is | Who must own it | Today |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Registrar account** | Where the domain is *registered* and renewed. Whoever holds it can move the domain. | Company login, company 2FA, company card | Namecheap (databayt.org), Cloudflare (balqalam.com), sdnic (mkan.sd) |
| **2. DNS zone** | Where MX, SPF, DKIM and DMARC live. Whoever edits it redirects all mail. | Company Cloudflare account | Cloudflare account `ce9a5376…`, all three |
| **3. Mail tenant / admin** | The provider account that creates mailboxes and grants access (Apple domain owner, Microsoft global admin, Google super admin) | A **dedicated company admin identity** (not a person's daily account) plus a sealed break-glass account | Namecheap Private Email (databayt.org) |
| **4. Mailbox permission** | Which human may read or send as `sales@` | Granted and revoked by layer 3 | — |

**With Apple**, layers 3 and 4 merge. The *domain owner* is whoever subscribed and added the
domain. *"Only the domain owner can add and remove people."* Members can *"only create and delete
your own email addresses"*, and every member's mail sits in their **own** account
(**Verified** [add/remove](https://support.apple.com/guide/icloud/add-or-remove-people-mmf0ccf838d5/icloud)).
The company Apple Account *can* stay the permanent domain owner, but any address it gives an
employee lives in the employee's mailbox.

**With Microsoft or Google**, layers 3 and 4 are separate by design. The tenant owns every mailbox,
and a person is only granted access.

## 6. Employee access model

| Model | Apple | Microsoft | Google |
|---|---|---|---|
| Employee uses the company account's password | ❌ violates the iCloud Terms §IV.A | ❌ not needed | ❌ not needed |
| Employee has their own account plus an address | ✅ but **mail stays with the employee** on removal | ✅ licensed mailbox, owned by the tenant | ✅ user, owned by the org |
| Several people work one `sales@` with shared history | ❌ no shared mailbox | ✅ **shared mailbox** (free ≤50 GB; each user needs a license) | ✅ **Group as collaborative inbox** |
| Access revocable without touching anyone's password | ⚠️ only through app-specific passwords (max 25, all revoked if the main password changes, [102654](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102654)) | ✅ remove the permission | ✅ remove from the group |

Sources (**Verified**): [Microsoft shared mailboxes](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/admin/email/about-shared-mailboxes),
[Google collaborative inbox](https://support.google.com/a/users/answer/167430).

## 7. Employee onboarding (Recommendation, Microsoft path)

```text
Admin creates  ali@databayt.org  (licensed, Plan 1)  → MFA enrolled on the employee's phone
  → grant Full Access + Send As on  sales@balqalam.com  (shared)
  → employee adds the account in Apple Mail (Mac + iPhone): Exchange, Microsoft sign-in
  → shared mailbox appears; replies go out "as" sales@balqalam.com
  → Twenty: employee's mailbox connected (Microsoft), owner set on their companies/opportunities
```

The employee never learns any password except their own.

## 8. Employee offboarding (Recommendation, Microsoft path)

| Step | Where | Handled by the provider? |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Block sign-in, reset the password, **revoke all sessions** | Microsoft 365 admin | ✅ [remove a former employee](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/admin/add-users/remove-former-employee-step-1) |
| 2. Remove Full Access and Send As on every shared mailbox | Exchange admin | ✅ |
| 3. Wipe or remove the account from their devices (Apple Mail, iPhone) | device / remote sign-out | ⚠️ the session revoke stops sync, but mail already downloaded stays on the device |
| 4. **Convert their personal mailbox to a shared mailbox**, so history stays with the company and the license frees up | Exchange admin | ✅ [convert](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/admin/email/convert-user-mailbox-to-shared-mailbox) |
| 5. Grant the replacement access to that converted mailbox, plus the shared `sales@` | Exchange admin | ✅ |
| 6. Check for **inbox forwarding rules** they set (exfiltration) | Exchange admin | ✅ but a manual check |
| 7. Reassign their Twenty records and deactivate their Twenty user | Twenty | ❌ outside email |
| 8. Remove them from Cloudflare, Namecheap and the Slack admin if they ever had access | each console | ❌ outside email |
| 9. Rotate any shared secret they saw (a break-glass password, API keys) | Keychain / `credentials` skill | ❌ outside email |

**With Apple**, step 4 does not exist. Removing a member deletes their addresses, bounces new
mail and *leaves their history in their own iCloud account* (**Verified**). Moving an address to another
member needs **both people to accept**, so an uncooperative leaver can block it
(**Verified** [transfer](https://support.apple.com/guide/icloud/transfer-custom-email-domain-addresses-mm043dbc8d94/icloud)).
Whether history moves with a transferred address is **Must-test** (Apple doesn't say).

## 9. Hostile employee scenario

A salesperson leaves on bad terms. What can they keep?

| Asset | Apple iCloud+ | Microsoft / Google | What protects it |
|---|---|---|---|
| The domain | Safe, unless they had registrar access | Safe, same condition | Registrar access for founders only, with 2FA and registrar lock (the tracer checks the lock) |
| DNS | Safe, unless they had Cloudflare access | Same | Cloudflare members audited; NS and MX drift alerts within 24 hours |
| The address `sales@` | Address deleted (the owner removes them), but new mail bounces until a new one is made | Kept; access is removed in minutes | Shared mailbox model |
| **Past customer mail** | **They keep it.** It is in their own iCloud account. | Stays in the tenant; anything synced to a device until revocation is the residual | Shared mailbox; Twenty sync |
| Incoming customer mail | Stops reaching them | Stops reaching them; check forwarding rules | Offboarding step 6 |
| Outgoing identity | Gone once the address is removed | Gone after Send As is removed | — |
| Account recovery | Risk if their phone was a trusted number on the company Apple Account | No risk if the admin identity was never shared | Company-held recovery (§12) |
| Customer list and pipeline | Whatever they exported | Whatever they exported | Twenty roles: sales can't bulk-export; reassign records |

No mail product stops someone who **already downloaded** mail. The defence is to keep the
history in a company system (Twenty plus company-owned mailboxes) and to revoke access fast.

## 10. Twenty CRM architecture

```text
Twenty (system of record)
  ├── companies · contacts · opportunities · tasks · workflow state
  ├── connected mailboxes ──sync── each salesperson's licensed mailbox (Microsoft OAuth / Google / IMAP)
  └── workflows ──HTTP Request / webhook──▶ kun automation (drafts, follow-ups, digests)
```

- Twenty connects **Google, Microsoft or IMAP/SMTP/CalDAV** accounts. IMAP arrived in v1.3.0 (Aug 2025).
  *"Only true mailboxes can be connected."* (**Verified** [docs](https://docs.twenty.com/user-guide/calendar-emails/overview), [releases](https://twenty.com/releases))
- Workflow **Send Email** goes to one recipient per action from a connected account. There are also an **HTTP Request**
  action, a **webhook trigger** and record webhooks (**Verified** [send from workflows](https://docs.twenty.com/user-guide/workflows/capabilities/send-emails-from-workflows),
  [actions](https://docs.twenty.com/user-guide/workflows/capabilities/workflow-actions)).
- **Must-test:** whether mail that arrives in a *shared* mailbox reaches Twenty. A shared mailbox isn't
  a "true mailbox", so it can't be connected. Two ways out:
  (a) make `sales@` a Microsoft 365 group or distribution list that delivers into each member's mailbox, which Twenty does sync; or
  (b) license `sales@` as a role mailbox ($48/yr), held by the admin, with the team delegated in.
- Apple mail could reach Twenty through IMAP with an app-specific password, but that is exactly the shared-credential
  pattern the iCloud Terms rule out for a team.

## 12. Security architecture

- **Admin identity:** one dedicated admin account per provider (for example `admin@databayt.org`).
  It is never used for daily mail. Its MFA uses a hardware key or an authenticator held by **two founders**, and
  there is a **break-glass** second admin with credentials sealed in the company Keychain (`credentials` skill).
- **Apple specifically** (if used anywhere): the trusted phone number must be a **company SIM**,
  not a person's. The recovery key needs an Apple device, and losing it means permanent lockout
  (**Verified** [109345](https://support.apple.com/en-us/109345)).
- **Registrar:** 2FA, registrar lock on (checked daily), company card, auto-renew on (verified monthly, see Health tracing).
- **DNS:** Cloudflare 2FA, least-privilege API tokens, **DNSSEC** to be turned on (Proposed, §19).
- **Phishing and takeover:** MFA for every user, Microsoft/Google default anti-phish, and no SMTP basic auth.
  Microsoft turns SMTP AUTH basic auth off by default at the end of December 2026
  ([timeline](https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/blog/exchange/updated-exchange-online-smtp-auth-basic-authentication-deprecation-timeline/4489835)),
  so automation uses OAuth or Graph.
- **Passwords:** Keychain (`credentials` skill), never in chat, `.env` or docs.

## 13. DNS / email authentication

Every value comes **from the provider's admin console**. Never copy values from this page or another domain.

| Record | Name | Shape | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Verification | `@` TXT | `MS=…` / `google-site-verification=…` / `apple-domain=…` | provider console |
| MX | `@` | Microsoft: `<domain-key>.mail.protection.outlook.com` · Google: `smtp.google.com` · Apple: `mx01/mx02.mail.icloud.com` | console / [Apple 102374](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102374) |
| SPF | `@` TXT | exactly **one** record: `v=spf1 include:<provider> ~all` (plus `include:` for each other legitimate sender) | console |
| DKIM | `selector1._domainkey` / `google._domainkey` / `sig1._domainkey` | CNAME or TXT from the console | console |
| DMARC | `_dmarc` TXT | start `v=DMARC1; p=none; rua=mailto:<reports>` → `quarantine` → `reject` | policy (Recommendation) |
| App mail | `send.<domain>` | Resend/SES SPF + DKIM on a subdomain, kept separate from people mail | Resend |
| Domains with no mail | `@` TXT + `_dmarc` | `v=spf1 -all` + `v=DMARC1; p=reject;` | anti-spoof |

**Current state** (what the tracer sees today):
- databayt.org: Private Email MX, SPF and DKIM are correct; DMARC is `quarantine` but has no `rua`.
- balqalam.com: Cloudflare Routing MX; DMARC is `p=none` with no `rua`.
- **mkan.sd: no records at all, so anyone can send as @mkan.sd.**

## 14. Deliverability and outreach

| Mail type | Volume | Where it must go out from |
|---|---|---|
| Human sales replies | tens a day | `sales@<product>`, via the provider |
| Automated sales outreach | hundreds a day | **A separate domain or subdomain** (for example `try-balqalam.com` or `go.balqalam.com`) through a sequencing tool, with its own SPF, DKIM and DMARC. **Never** `hi@`/`sales@`. |
| Transactional (OTP, invites) | app-driven | Resend/SES on `send.<domain>`, as Hogwarts already does on databayt.org |
| Marketing newsletters | batch | an ESP with one-click unsubscribe |

Rules from the receivers (**Verified**):
- Gmail and Yahoo require SPF, DKIM, DMARC, one-click unsubscribe, and a spam rate under 0.3%. Gmail has rejected non-compliant mail since November 2025 ([Google 81126](https://support.google.com/a/answer/81126)).
- Outlook.com requires the same for senders over 5,000 a day from 5 May 2025 ([Microsoft](https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/blog/microsoftdefenderforoffice365blog/strengthening-email-ecosystem-outlook%E2%80%99s-new-requirements-for-high%E2%80%90volume-senders/4399730)).

Provider caps:
- **iCloud:** 1,000 a day, with bulk prohibited.
- **Google:** 2,000 messages a day and 3,000 external recipients ([limits](https://knowledge.workspace.google.com/admin/gmail/gmail-sending-limits-in-google-workspace)).
- **Exchange:** 10,000 recipients a day plus a tenant-wide external limit. Microsoft tells bulk senders to use third parties ([limits](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/office365/servicedescriptions/exchange-online-service-description/exchange-online-limits)).

**None of the three is an outreach engine.** Protecting the main domains' reputation is the point.

**Decision checklist:**

- **Choose Apple** only for a brand run by **one person** who will never hand the inbox to someone else.

## 19. Migration plan

Nothing touches production before step 4.

1. **Decide the provider** (Abdout) and confirm billing-country availability.
2. **Create the tenant** with the admin and break-glass identities. Founder-held MFA.
3. **Lab test on `lab.databayt.org`.** Add only new records under that subdomain. The apex MX is untouched.
   Create `test@lab.databayt.org` (shared) and one licensed test user, then run the checklist in §20.
4. **balqalam.com first.** Snapshot the zone to `cf/dns/balqalam-com-snapshot-<date>.json`, set the MX TTL to 300
   the day before, add the verification and DKIM records, cut over the MX and SPF, and keep DMARC `p=none` with `rua`.
   Update `cf/domains.json` in the **same commit** so the tracer adopts the new baseline.
5. **databayt.org next.** Export the Private Email mailboxes over IMAP into the new tenant, then cut over. Cancel Private Email at its renewal.
6. **mkan.sd and product-4.** Fresh setup.

## 21. Rollback plan

- Before any cutover, the full zone is saved to `cf/dns/<domain>-snapshot-<date>.json`, and the old provider stays paid until the end of the month.
- **Rollback = restore the MX, SPF and DKIM records from the snapshot.** With TTL 300, inbound returns to the old provider within minutes.
  Mail that arrived at the new provider in the meantime is exported over IMAP and imported back.
- The tracer flags any half-done state (MX drift is a blocking alert), so a stalled migration can't go unnoticed.

| ⚠️ warning | expires in 90 days or less · auto-renew not verified in the last 35 days · registrar lock off · DMARC `p=none` or no `rua` · a no-mail domain that can be spoofed · TLS expires in 14 days or less · probe unreachable |

**Why auto-renew needs a human:** no registry or whois shows auto-renew, and Namecheap and
sdnic offer no API we hold. So someone (Cowork, in Abdout's Chrome) looks at each registrar page
once a month, and `autoRenewVerifiedAt` in `cf/domains.json` records that they did. A stale stamp is
a warning, and the warning *is* the reminder.
