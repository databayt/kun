## The limit, first

In the Quran, «كُن فَيَكُونُ» describes how God brings a thing into being:

> «إِنَّمَا أَمْرُهُ إِذَا أَرَادَ شَيْئًا أَن يَقُولَ لَهُ كُن فَيَكُونُ» (يس 36:82)

That is His alone, and nothing here claims any part of it. The engine borrows only the grammar of the sentence: a thing is said once, clearly, and the work follows from what was said. A person says a word; the tools do what the word already means.

## Kun, the word

Kun is what should be, declared once and kept in git: the [keywords](/docs/keywords), the [lane table](/docs/lanes), the mandate, the skills and the rules. It costs nothing while it rests. Changing a declaration changes everything that follows from it, and nothing else has to be told.

## Gabriel, the messenger

In the Islamic narrative, Gabriel (جبريل) carries the word to the one who is to act on it. The engine's messenger takes his name from that role and from nothing more: he is a command, [`gabriel`](/docs/gabriel), that carries a request to whoever runs it and brings the answer back.

Six things the narrative says about the messenger are used here as requirements. Each one can be checked.

| The narrative | What the engine must do |
| --- | --- |
| **Faithful.** «نَزَلَ بِهِ الرُّوحُ الْأَمِينُ» (الشعراء 26:193); «مُّطَاعٍ ثَمَّ أَمِينٍ» (التكوير 81:21) | Deliver the command as it was given, and report only what the evidence shows. Every report cites a ledger line, a commit or a URL; a run without evidence is marked unverified. |
| **By command only.** «وَمَا نَتَنَزَّلُ إِلَّا بِأَمْرِ رَبِّكَ» (مريم 19:64) | Never start spending, publishing, deploying or messaging a person on his own. This is the mandate. |
| **Strong.** «ذِي قُوَّةٍ عِندَ ذِي الْعَرْشِ مَكِينٍ» (التكوير 81:20); «عَلَّمَهُ شَدِيدُ الْقُوَىٰ» (النجم 53:5) | Carry long work to its end: a budget, a progress note, and a run that resumes where it stopped. |
| **Obeyed.** «مُّطَاعٍ ثَمَّ أَمِينٍ» (التكوير 81:21) | One coordinator. A worker takes its lane from the table and does not renegotiate it. |
| **Comes in a form the person can receive.** «فَأَرْسَلْنَا إِلَيْهَا رُوحَنَا فَتَمَثَّلَ لَهَا بَشَرًا سَوِيًّا» (مريم 19:17) | Reach people where they are: the phone, WhatsApp, Slack, the terminal. Arabic first. |
| **Teaches by asking.** The hadith of Gabriel (Sahih Muslim, from Umar ibn al-Khattab; also in Sahih al-Bukhari): a man in pure white clothes sat with the Prophet ﷺ and asked about Islam, Iman and Ihsan, so that those listening would learn | Before starting, ask the few questions whose answers change the work. |

The verses are quoted from the Quran.com text. Their readings here follow the mainstream tafsir, which takes الروح الأمين and شديد القوى to be Gabriel.

## Two vocabularies, kept apart

The spell names in the keyword book come from Harry Potter. They are a playful way to remember keywords and nothing else. This page is the engine's identity. The two are never mixed: no page describes Gabriel in the language of spells.
