# Copy

[Brand](/docs/brand) says what our voice *is*. [Strategy](/docs/social/strategy) says what wins.
This page is the **craft bar** — the checks a draft must clear before a human ever sees it, and
the ones a human rejects it against. `/draft` writes to it, `/carousel` writes to it, `/approve`
reads it, and the 5-minute drain reads it before it answers the first ask of the run.

It exists because the pipeline could already stop a *false* post and could not stop a *boring*
one. On 2026-08-05 the unattended drain produced this, and it passed every gate the pipeline had:

> `القبول في مدرستك… من أول سؤال ولي الأمر إلى أول يوم للطالب.`
> — followed by five bullets, and a trust line that was tenant scoping translated into Arabic.

One core piece, inside budget, no invented metric, no untagged link, correct Arabic. It was a
summary of `admission.mdx`. Nothing in the pipeline was looking at whether anyone would read it.

## The seven checks

Named and binary, so a skill, a reviewer, and a dismiss reason can all say **"fails check 3."**

### 1. Hook

Cover everything but the first line. Read only that. Does it name a pain the reader has felt, or
a promise they want? Naming the product, the feature, or the category is a **description**, and a
description is not a hook. Maximum 12 words. Never opens with the brand name.

- **Fails** — `القبول في مدرستك… من أول سؤال ولي الأمر إلى أول يوم للطالب.` (a table of contents)
- **Passes** — `أسبوع المدير يذوب في الورق.` · `آخر الفصل مشهد يعرفه كل معلم.`

### 2. One idea

The piece makes one claim. Cut it at any sentence boundary — if both halves stand as posts, it is
two posts. **A bulleted feature list is the failure signature.** Bullets are allowed only when
each is a step in one process and there are three or fewer.

### 3. Specificity

Name the thing, not its category. `القبول، الحضور، الفواتير` beats `العمليات الإدارية`. Every
abstraction is replaced with the object itself or deleted. The piece carries at least one image a
person can actually see: a desk, a corridor, a stack of paper, a Thursday night.

### 4. Scene

Somewhere in the first third, the reader recognizes their own week. This is the check that
separates copy that reads well from copy that reads like documentation — and the one the drain
has never passed, structurally: **product docs contain no Thursdays.** If the piece was written
from a feature list, it will fail here no matter how good the sentences are.

### 5. Sibling

Read Arabic and English side by side. If they are line-for-line parallel with the same clause
order, one is a translation and the doctrine is broken. They may differ in length, in what they
lead with, and in which detail they keep. **Divergence in at least two places is the floor.**

### 6. CTA

One verb, one destination, last. Never two asks. If there is a link it carries UTM (see
`src/lib/social-utm.ts` — `applyUtm` runs at delivery and is idempotent). If there is no link,
say what to do instead. Never both.

### 7. Portability

The core piece must survive any channel without editing:

- the first line works **cold** — no image, no context
- the body reads with **no link preview**
- hashtags sit at the end, 3 maximum, so a channel that ignores them loses nothing
- **no platform-specific verb** — no "swipe", no "link in bio", no "tap the highlight"

A piece that passes this is forwardable on WhatsApp, which [Strategy](/docs/social/strategy) names
as the viral unit for Sudan. At our volume, a portable core piece **is** the per-channel
adaptation that matters — which is why the queue lane writes one piece rather than a thin fan-out.

## The reject list

Auto-fail. No discussion:

- Opens with the brand name, `نحن`, or "We're excited to announce"
- A bulleted feature list of four or more items
- A number, name, price, or date not present in the brief
- More than 3 hashtags — or any hashtags on Telegram, WhatsApp, or in a LinkedIn body
- Emoji in the first line; more than one anywhere; ever as a bullet marker
- An exclamation mark; more than one question mark
- Engineering vocabulary carried into Arabic — `نطاق المستأجر`, `كل قراءة وكتابة`, `واجهة برمجية`

**Length.** The core piece lands at **400–900 characters of Arabic**. Under 400 only for X. Over
900 means check 2 already failed and you have not noticed yet.

## The Arabic register ladder

Four rungs. Rung 1 is the failure mode; rung 2 is the house default. "Correct Arabic" is not the
bar — a press release is correct Arabic.

### Rung 1 — فصحى الصحافة (MSA press-release). Never ships.

Markers you can point at:

- Agentless passives where an actor exists: `يتم إرسال`, `تم إطلاق`, `يُنشأ`
- Nominalized verb chains, مصدر stacking: `عملية تحسين إدارة العمليات`
- Institutional self-reference: `تفخر … بأن تعلن`, `في إطار حرصنا على`, `انطلاقًا من`
- Abstraction nouns with no referent: `الحلول`, `المنظومة`, `التحول الرقمي`, `الكفاءة التشغيلية`
- Adjective pairs used for rhythm: `الشامل والمتكامل`, `الفعّال والمرن`
- Sentences past ~20 words carrying three إضافة chains

**The passive rule** — the distinction that makes this operational, drawn from our own corpus:

- `الدرجة تُرصد مرة واحدة` — **good.** The sentence is *about the mark*. The passive puts the
  subject where the reader's attention already is.
- `يُنشأ ملف الطالب وأولياء الأمر والرسوم والفواتير` — **bad.** The reader wants to know who does
  this and whether it costs them anything. The passive hides exactly that.

> Passive is right when the object is the topic.
> Passive is wrong when it hides an actor the reader is asking about.

### Rung 2 — فصحى معاصرة مبسّطة. The house default.

- Active verbs with a named actor
- One clause per sentence; a dash or a colon carries the turn
- Nouns the reader touches: `الورق`, `الدفتر`, `الكشف`, `الفاتورة`, `المكتب`, `الطابور`
- Everyday verbs — `يذوب`, `يضيع`, `يصل`, `ينتهي` — not `يتسنّى`, `يغدو`, `يُضحي`
- Grammatically MSA, no dialect morphology, but the **rhythm of speech**

This is what our shipped decks already are. It is also the only rung that reads correct to a
Saudi, an Emirati, **and** a Sudanese reader at once — which is exactly what hogwarts needs, since
its audience spans all three.

### Rung 3 — عامية بيضاء (pan-Arab white colloquial).

MSA skeleton, spoken vocabulary and connectives, no morphology that marks one country. Second
person (`تعرف`, `جرّب`, `خليك`), conversational turns (`طيب`, `ببساطة`). Consumer and short-form
only.

### Rung 4 — لهجة محلية.

A named local dialect. Sudanese for sijillee, per its own brand page: *"a helpful neighbour who
happens to be good with books."* Only where a brand doc names it, only on that brand's channels.

### The map

| Brand | Channels | Rung | Why |
|-------|----------|------|-----|
| **hogwarts** | LinkedIn, Facebook, X | **2** | The buyer is a principal on a phone. Rung 3 reads unserious to an institution. |
| **hogwarts** | Instagram, WhatsApp | **2, warmed** — second person allowed | Parents. Keep MSA morphology, drop institutional distance. |
| **mkan** | IG, TikTok, Snapchat, FB | **3** | Consumer, Saudi-leaning. Imagery leads; copy is short and warm. |
| **databayt** | X, LinkedIn, Telegram | **2** in Arabic; English may run technical | Developers. Precision over warmth. |
| **sijillee** | FB, WhatsApp, Telegram | **4 — Sudanese** | Its brand page says so. Humility in tone; never crisis-adjacent. |
| **moallimee** | — | **2, warmed** | Pre-launch. Locks with positioning. |

### Script and typography

- **Latin digits** (1, 2, 3) in copy — Gulf marketing norm. Applies to captions, not just decks.
- **Diacritics** only to disambiguate, never decorative — and never in a Thmanyah display headline
  (clearance is a known render issue; see [Carousel](/docs/social/carousel)).
- **Never letter-space Arabic.** It is a connected script.
- **The brand name in Arabic prose: transliterate.** `في هوجورتس`, not `في hogwarts`. Keep the
  Latin form for handles, links, and hashtags. A Latin token mid-sentence forces an RTL reader to
  context-switch, and the repo has been doing both — this line settles it.

## The wordlist

| Banned (AR) | Use instead |
|-------------|-------------|
| الحلول · حلول متكاملة | the actual thing — `نظام القبول`, `كشف الدرجات` |
| المنظومة | `النظام`, or name the module |
| التحول الرقمي | what changes — `من الورق إلى الشاشة` |
| تفخر · يسرّنا أن نعلن · في إطار | delete the sentence |
| يتم + مصدر (`يتم إرسال`) | the active verb — `يرسل النظام` |
| الكفاءة · الفعالية | the time or the error actually saved |
| ثورة · نقلة نوعية | nothing. We cannot make that claim. |
| سهل الاستخدام | show it (`خمس خطوات`) instead of claiming it |
| عزيزي ولي الأمر (as an opener) | speak to them, don't address them |

**Preferred** — the register's own vocabulary, mined from the copy that works:
`الورق` · `الدفتر` · `الكشف` · `الفاتورة` · `الطابور` · `يذوب` · `يضيع` · `يُنجز` ·
`ينتهي بهدوء` · `أول يوم` · `آخر الفصل` · `أسبوع المدير` · `مرة واحدة` · `في مكان واحد` ·
`بلمحة` · `بنقرة`.

**Banned (EN):** revolutionize · seamless · cutting-edge · empower · streamline · solutions ·
leverage · game-changer · "Excited to announce" · 🚀 · "workflows" (name them) · "AI-powered"
unless the post is actually about the AI.

## Three angles before one draft

Before writing, name three angles in one line each:

- **The pain** — what the reader's week costs them
- **The moment** — a scene they recognize
- **The proof** — the one true fact from the brief

Pick the one whose first line survives check 1. Say in one clause why the other two lost. Then
write only the winner. The runner-ups are not persisted anywhere — the discipline produces the
hook; the artifact has no reader. A hand-run session says them out loud; the drain prints them to
its log.

## Direction: the three knobs

A brief says what the post is about. The knobs say how to write it — and they are this page's own
vocabulary, so a contributor sets the same things a reviewer dismisses against.

| Knob | Values | Unset means |
|------|--------|-------------|
| **Angle** | pain · moment · proof | The writer runs the three-angle discipline itself |
| **Register** | rung 2 · 3 · 4 | Take the rung the brand + channel map above prescribes |
| **Like this one** | a prior post for the same brand | No reference |

Two rules make them worth having:

- **A set knob is a decision, not a hint.** The writer still names three angles in its log, but it
  writes the one asked for. Substituting a better idea silently is how direction stops being worth
  giving.
- **Rung 1 is storable but never askable.** The column means "a rung on the ladder"; the Hub offers
  2–4, because offering the rung this page says never ships would return a draft already failed.

A reference is matched for **voice** — rhythm, sentence length, distance from the reader — never
for subject, and its sentences are never reused. `content/social/pillars.json` has been encoding
Angle and Register inside its brief strings since the seed lane shipped; the knobs are that
convention given a home the UI can reach.

## Refinement: a draft is a conversation

A draft that fails a check does not have to be dismissed and re-asked. Typing what to change in
the Hub's agent window files the **next turn** of the same thread.

- The parent is **superseded**, not overwritten — every turn keeps its own copy, its own media,
  and its own approvability, so a v3 that came out worse never destroys the v2 someone liked.
- The **root brief travels unchanged**; the instruction rides beside it. Folding the instruction
  into the brief would lose the difference between what the post is about and what was wrong with
  the last attempt — by turn three the brief would be a changelog.
- A refinement **changes only what was asked**. Keep the hook if the note is about length; keep the
  length if the note is about the hook. Quietly rewriting the whole post takes away the part the
  reviewer had already accepted, and they have to find it again.
- Only the newest turn is `answered`, so the review queue shows the current draft rather than three
  versions of one post competing for a slot.

The reviewer's dismiss is still the feedback loop, not the quality gate — a draft that fails
check 1 or check 2 is rewritten before it is answered, not after.

## Why not a candidate set, an A/B, or a judge

`SocialDraftRequest` holds exactly one `ar` and one `en` **per turn**, and that is deliberate at
this volume. A thread is a sequence a human steered, not a set to choose from.

- **Persisted candidates** would have to ride the `note` field, which is rendered *verbatim to the
  asker* in the agent window — runner-ups would be invisible where they matter and corrupting
  where they are visible. Refinement gets the same outcome by asking, and records who asked.
- **Attribution columns** (`hook`, `pillar` on a variant) need six months to hold fifty rows at ~2
  posts/week — below any threshold that distinguishes a good hook from a lucky Tuesday. The
  strategy's stop-loss fires at 3 months on *presence*, not attribution. (`angle` became a column
  anyway — as an **input**, which needs no sample size to be useful.)
- **An LLM judge** is the L4 gate, behind `/decide`. It costs a second model call per draft on a
  subscription pool that already hits session limits. The human gate exists and takes twenty
  seconds.

The feedback loop we run is free, and since 2026-08-06 it is actually **read**: dismiss reasons are
stored as a structured `dismissReason`, and `node scripts/social-drafts.mjs lessons` shows the
drain what reviewers rejected in the last 60 days, most common failure first — before it writes the
first draft of a run. Until that column existed the reason was concatenated into a prose `note`
where nothing could group it, and this paragraph described an intention rather than a mechanism.
If "no hook" stops appearing in dismiss reasons, this page worked.
