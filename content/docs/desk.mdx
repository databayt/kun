The [hardware](/docs/hardware) page puts a Mac Studio and a Studio Display **on the desk**.
This page builds that desk: a large solid-wood top in the Uplift style on a black steel
frame welded at one fixed height. There is no crank, motor or anything else that moves,
so there is nothing to wear out.

![Finished desk: 200 by 75 cm hardwood top on two black steel T-legs, 74 cm high](/desk/desk-overview.svg)

## How it goes together

![Exploded view: top with eight M6 inserts, two welded T-legs, bolted crossbeam, four leveling glides](/desk/desk-exploded.svg)

There are three parts, made by three people, and they meet only at the end:

1. **The top.** You glue 12 hardwood strips into two panels, join the panels, then
   flatten and varnish the result.
2. **The frame.** A welder at Gakinjiro builds it from the drawings on this page.
3. **Assembly.** Eight M6 bolts screw into threaded inserts in the underside of the top.
   Leveling glides go under the feet.

The frame bolts to the top and the crossbeam bolts to the legs, so the desk comes apart
into four flat pieces for moving.

## Choose the height

Do this before the welder cuts anything. The leg column is the only part that changes.

| Use | Desk height | Column length |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Seated (default) | 74 cm | 62 cm |
| Seated, tall person (185 cm+) | 76 cm | 64 cm |
| Standing, fixed | your standing elbow height, usually 100–110 cm | 62 + (height − 74) cm |

The column length is the desk height minus the top (3.8), the arm (4), the foot (4)
and the glide (0.5).

## Build

### Timeline

| When | You | The welder |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Day 1 | Buy timber (A), have it ripped and planed, take it home | Order the frame with the drawings from step 3 |
| Days 1–21 | Wood dries indoors. Buy B–F | Builds the frame (about 1 week) |
| Day 22 | Glue both panels (step 1) | |
| Day 23 | Join, flatten, cut (step 2) | Delivers the frame |
| Days 24–26 | Sand and varnish (step 4) | |
| Day 28 | Assembly (step 5) | |

### 1 · Strips and panels

![Each 15 by 5 cm board is ripped in two and planed to 6.5 by 4 cm strips](/desk/desk-strips.svg)

1. At the yard, have each of the 7 boards ripped in half and planed on all four faces to
   **6.5 × 4 cm**. That gives 14 strips: 12 for the top and 2 spare. Reject any strip that
   is twisted or cracked.
2. At home, stack the strips flat with pine spacers between the layers, indoors and off
   the floor, for **2–3 weeks**. Wood from Kigali yards is often still wet, and wet wood
   glued now will crack or cup later.
3. Lay out 6 strips per panel. **Flip every other strip** so the end-grain arcs alternate
   up and down, which keeps the panel from cupping. Number the strips with chalk.

![Glue-up of six strips with alternating growth rings and alternating clamps every 25 cm](/desk/desk-glueup.svg)

4. Dry-fit first, with no glue. Clamps go every 25 cm, alternating above and below the
   panel. Put a taped pine caul across each end to keep the panel flat. When the dry fit
   closes with no gaps, take it apart.
5. Glue: run a thin bead along each joint, spread it, then clamp **just until a line of
   small beads squeezes out**. Too tight starves the joint. Check the panel is flat with
   the straightedge.
6. After ~1 hour, scrape off the half-dry squeeze-out with the glue scraper. Leave the
   panel clamped overnight. Make the second panel the same way.

### 2 · Join, flatten, cut

![Two panels joined with dowels every 20 cm, then cut to 200 by 75 cm](/desk/desk-join.svg)

1. Scrape and sand both panels flat with 80 grit. Check across and along with the
   straightedge. Low spots show as light under it.
2. Line up the two panels and mark a dowel every 20 cm along the joint (10 dowels). Drill
   20 mm deep into each edge with the 10 mm bit **in the dowel jig**, so the holes line
   up exactly.
3. Glue the edges and dowels, clamp, and check flatness across the joint. Leave it
   overnight.
4. Take the top back to the yard and have it cut **to 200 cm long and 75 cm deep**.
   The joined panels come out about 205 × 78. This cut needs a long, straight saw, which
   is why you don't do it at home.
5. Round all edges slightly with the sander and 80 grit, then soften them with 120.

### 3 · The frame (welder)

Give the fabricator these two drawings. They contain everything needed: tube sizes,
lengths and hole positions.

![T-leg drawing: 70 cm foot, 62 cm column, 65 cm arm with slotted holes, M8 nuts and crossbeam plate](/desk/desk-tleg.svg)

![Front view: 200 cm top, legs 160 cm apart, crossbeam 156 cm, height 74 cm](/desk/desk-front.svg)

What to tell the welder:

- Make **two T-legs**: a 60×40 foot 70 cm long, an 80×40 column 62 cm tall and a 40×40 arm
  65 cm long. The column is centred on both the foot and the arm. Every joint is 90°,
  checked both ways before the final weld.
- Weld an **M8 nut inside each end of both feet**, to take the leveling glides.
- Drill **4 slotted Ø7 mm holes** through each arm (12 mm long slots, along the arm) at
  10, 27, 48 and 65 cm from the front. The slots let the wood move with the seasons.
- Weld a **100 × 60 × 5 plate** on the inner face of each column, 8 cm below the top of
  the arm. **Bolt** the 156 cm 60×40 crossbeam to the plates with M8 bolts. Don't weld it,
  so the desk can come apart.
- Grind all welds smooth, fit the end caps, then prime and paint matte black (or powder
  coat).

Check before you pay: the legs stand without rocking on a flat floor, the arms are level,
and the two legs are identical.

### 4 · Finish the top

1. Sand the whole top 120 → 180 → 240, wiping off the dust between grits.
2. Turn it over and drill the two **60 mm grommet holes**: 40 cm in from each end and
   9 cm from the back edge. Drill from the top face until the bit's point shows, then
   finish from the underside so the face doesn't splinter.
3. Fill any small defects with filler, let it dry and sand flush.
4. Varnish **both faces and all edges**. If only one face is sealed, the top cups.
   - Coat 1: varnish thinned ~10 % with white spirit. Let it dry, then sand lightly with 240.
   - Coats 2 and 3: full strength, with a light 240 sand between them.
   - Let it cure 3 days before it takes weight.

### 5 · Assemble

![Underside layout: arm positions, eight M6 insert points, two 60 mm grommet holes and the cable tray](/desk/desk-underside.svg)

1. Lay the top **face down** on a blanket. Bolt the crossbeam to the legs and stand the
   frame upside down on the top. Centre it: the legs are 20 cm in from each end, and the
   arms start 5 cm from the front edge.
2. Mark the 8 slot centres with the pencil. Lift the frame off.
3. Drill each pilot hole **30 mm deep**, with tape on the bit as a depth stop so it never
   comes through. Drive in the M6 inserts with the hex key, keeping them straight.
4. Put the frame back and bolt it on with the M6 × 35 bolts and washers. Make them snug,
   not crushing.
5. Screw the glides into the feet, turn the desk upright (two people), and level it with
   the straightedge across the top in both directions.
6. Fit the grommets, screw the cable tray under the back between the legs, and route
   the cables.

## Check before you call it done

- [ ] No rocking on the floor, and the top is level both ways
- [ ] Lean 50 kg on a front corner: no sway or creak
- [ ] No glue spots on the face (they show as dull patches under varnish)
- [ ] Every bolt is snug, and no insert has pulled out
- [ ] The varnish has cured for 3 days before the monitor and Mac go on
